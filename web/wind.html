AN IDEALIZED WIND EXPERIMENT

Wind becomes force.

Double the wind speed and the same simple drag model gives four times the force. Move a probe through an idealized vortex to see the relationship. All settings here are assumptions, independent of the historical storms in the atlas.

THE MODEL

A useful shape, with deliberate limits.

Inside the core, swirl speed rises linearly from zero. Outside, it falls as the inverse of distance. This is a Rankine vortex: a simple analytic velocity field. It has no inflow, updraft, terrain, gusts, multiple vortices, or evolving storm structure.

Arrow direction shows the horizontal flow. The pressure readout is dynamic pressure, not the atmospheric pressure deficit inside a tornado. Tracers start paused and pause again when the page is hidden.

FROM WIND TO DAMAGE

Force is only one part of failure.

This experiment calculates a generic drag force. It has no building geometry, connections, materials, internal pressure, debris impacts, or failure criteria. It cannot assign a damage rating, reconstruct a building failure, or determine whether a structure is safe.

Real damage assessments require evidence about the affected objects. Explore the El Reno survey photographs to see the documented damage separately from this experiment.
