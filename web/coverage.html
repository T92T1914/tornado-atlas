  • How the aerial survey defined the damage envelope

    Wakimoto and colleagues combined June 1 and 3, 2013 ground surveys with a June 4 aerial survey. A Cessna 172 flew at 300 and 1200 m above ground and captured 1249 photographs. Where assessments differed, the ground estimate determined the assigned EF value. Their EF0 damage envelope reached roughly 7 km just east of Highway 81. They explicitly separate that envelope from the El Reno tornado alone: a separate anticyclonic tornado extended damage southeast, and the southern edge suggests rear-flank downdraft damage.

    The roughly 7 km envelope does not replace the NWS 2.6-mile (about 4.2 km) maximum tornado width or final EF3 damage rating. Atlas links the paper without reproducing its figures. These survey methods establish no new Atlas coordinates, synchronized footage or registered appearance.
