Put the storm's scale beside its effects.

The visible funnel, the reported maximum tornado width and the surveyed damage describe different parts of this event. Compare their evidence here while keeping your selected replay moment.

Three sourced lengths, with their definitions.

The bars share a 5 km length reference. They compare reported magnitudes, not three footprints or the tornado's size at the current clock time.

  1. About 500 m: visible funnel

    Wakimoto and colleagues estimate the width at 2° elevation in their 23:24:38 to 23:24:50 UTC analysis. The northern edge was difficult to discern. Read section 4a and its limits.

  2. About 800 m: visible funnel

    The authors report this approximate diameter for the next analysis, 23:24:54 to 23:25:06 UTC. That passage does not repeat the earlier elevation definition. Read section 4b.

  3. 2.6 miles, about 4.2 km: reported event maximum

    The NWS reports the tornado's maximum width. This is a different quantity from the paper's visible-funnel estimates and is not assigned to every replay instant. Read the NWS event account.

The paper's analysis periods are source context, not continuously reconstructed appearance. No width is assigned to its final 23:25:09 photograph. The scene's 2 km grid provides a ground reference. WHAT STILL NEEDS EVIDENCE

The appearance of the storm.

A center path does not give the funnel's changing dimensions, rain curtains or subvortex motion. Those need registered photographs or footage. This scene therefore does not claim to reproduce what a particular witness saw, or show when a building failed.
