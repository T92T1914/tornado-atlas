KANSAS / 2007

Kiowa and Comanche Counties, Kansas

Greensburg May 4, 2007

On May 4, 2007, a tornado crossed rural Comanche County and moved into Kiowa County, devastating Greensburg. Its maximum damage rating was EF5. The retained National Centers for Environmental Information (NCEI) record reports 11 direct deaths and 63 direct injuries.

Follow the reported path through its communities, the warnings that preceded arrival and the work that continued after the storm. Two county records and later National Weather Service (NWS) accounts retain their individual times and definitions. Open the evidence dossier.

From Comanche County into Greensburg

The Comanche County record identifies the beginning of the tornado that later struck Greensburg. It describes damage to oil tanks and trees, with oil spread over pastures and a county road. The source assigns EF1 damage to those objects. Its explicit continuation into Kiowa County identifies one tornado across the county line.

South of Greensburg, the Kiowa account describes destroyed homes and a church. The tornado entered the south side of town at 9:45 p.m. in the NWS retrospective. The record describes destruction across nearly 95 percent of Greensburg and reports 961 homes and businesses destroyed, 216 with major damage and 307 with minor damage. The town's water tower, beside the Big Well, was among the structures lost. County records · NWS retrospective.

The Kiowa narrative follows a turn toward the north and northwest, then west, south and east as the tornado weakened northwest of Greensburg. It attributes that looping interpretation to radar and the ground survey. A new circulation was growing northeast of town while this tornado weakened. That later circulation belongs to the wider storm account and is not automatically joined to the Greensburg tornado here. Read the ending account and its limits.

Warnings before arrival

The NWS retrospective distinguishes three moments. The first warning concerned the tornado that eventually reached Greensburg. A later warning specifically named the town. Arrival at the south side of town was another event.

  1. 8:55 p.m. First warning associated with the tornado.
  2. 9:19 p.m. First warning specifically mentioning Greensburg.
  3. 9:45 p.m. Reported arrival at the south side of town.

The reported 26 minutes of lead time compares the town-specific warning with arrival. The passage does not print a time zone beside these evening labels. Warning issuance alone does not establish when each resident received the message or acted. NWS warning chronology.

A later official NWS directive reproduces a tornado-emergency statement labeled 9:41 p.m. CDT. It is available as a document example, with that later edition identified. It is not a recovered copy of the original transmission. Read the reproduced statement.

County records and clocks

The two source records describe different portions of the same tornado. Their labels use Central Standard Time with an explicit six-hour offset from UTC, written CST-6 in the source. May's local daylight clock was one hour ahead of those standard-time labels.

Reported locations and dimensions

The written continuation and matching boundary minute support the event association. The coordinates disagree: the Comanche record ends at longitude -99.4517, latitude 37.3668, while Kiowa begins at longitude -99.3826, latitude 37.3934. Both are retained. No connecting segment is drawn to conceal the difference. Compare the endpoint fields.

The county lengths are 3.01 and 25.8 miles. The NWS anniversary fact sheet separately reports a whole path of 28.8 miles, a duration of 65 minutes and a maximum width of 1.7 miles. The Kiowa width field is 3,000 yards. Each quantity keeps its original scope and rounding. The fact sheet's approximately 205 mph figure is an estimate, and a damage width does not establish the diameter of the visible funnel. NWS fact sheet.

Response and hazardous material

The aftermath involved damaged infrastructure and hazardous material as well as collapsed buildings. The retained Kiowa account describes oil-storage damage, debris removal and the main highway remaining closed for a month. Its dollar loss is a nominal historical estimate, not a present-day rebuilding price.

EPA reports sending response staff on May 5. Early work included fuel releases, chlorine cylinders, anhydrous ammonia and propane tanks. On May 9, a FEMA assignment covered oil, hazardous material, household hazardous waste and discarded appliances. Curbside collection began on May 11. These dates describe stages of response; the inactive profile does not establish when every property became safe. EPA response account.

Dated stages of recovery

The NWS/FEMA table dated April 25, 2008 reports 800,000 cubic yards of debris removed. The earlier Kiowa narrative reports about 400,000 cubic yards in its July 26 context. These are retained as differently dated accounts, without turning them into a continuous or complete cleanup measurement. The NWS anniversary account also describes utility reconstruction. One-year recovery report.

The city describes community meetings and a recovery plan guided by a FEMA team. The NWS gallery shows selected later stages through its captions: a new water tower in May 2008, a Main Street view dated May 5, 2012 and places with little new construction. These reports and photographs do not establish that every displaced resident returned or that recovery was complete. City recovery account · NWS documentary gallery.

Counts and remembrance

The Kiowa event record reports 11 direct deaths and 63 direct injuries. Its final note says the eleventh victim died on September 19, 2007 from a head injury sustained during the tornado. The city page separately reports ten deaths within city limits and deaths in other counties. Their scopes remain distinct. Those other-county figures are not added to this event record.
