# Atkins and colleagues: the Moore funnel against the damage path

[An Integrated Damage, Visual, and Radar Analysis of the 2013 Moore, Oklahoma, EF5 Tornado](https://doi.org/10.1175/BAMS-D-14-00033.1) by Nolan T. Atkins, Kelly M. Butler, Kayla R. Flynn and Roger M. Wakimoto, Bulletin of the American Meteorological Society 95(10), pages 1549 to 1561, published October 1, 2014. No file in `research`, `docs` or `exhibits` cited it before this record.

The question is narrow. What does the paper say about the condensation funnel of the Moore tornado of May 20, 2013 compared with its damage path, and where does it say it? The Newcastle and Moore dossier already says that a reported damage width is not the size of the visible funnel. This record checks that rule against a paper that measured both.

## How the paper sizes the funnel

Section "Photogrammetry" says the photogrammetry teams and mobile research radars "did not deploy on the Moore storm since it would have required deployment in urban areas". Footnote 2 on that sentence names one exception: the University of Oklahoma's mobile PX-1000 radar did deploy, as Kurdzo and colleagues (2014) summarize. The images came from "amateur storm chasers and news crews" through social media. Triangulation from two places at once "is not possible with the available photos of the Moore tornado." So each funnel diameter comes from one camera, from the angle across the funnel and the range to it, and the range rests on an assumption: "it is assumed that the condensation funnel center is collocated with the damage path centerline."

The paper states its error budget. The azimuth grid is good to 0.1°. The angle across the funnel is good to 0.2°. Photographer positions were checked by a site survey and are good to 1.5 m, the damage path position to 10 m, so the range is good to about 11.5 m.

The abstract's EF1 to EF3 summary is the paper's own generalization. The text near the start of the path names EF1 and EF2, and the text near the schools places EF3 and EF4 inside the funnel near the centerline. The paper prints no funnel diameter in metres in its text. The sizes are drawn in the figures: Fig. 2 shows the funnel size with its uncertainty as shaded areas, Figs. 4 and 5 are drawn the same way, and Fig. 3 shows funnel sizes as circles on the radar maps and as arrows on the photographs. This record did not read the figures.

## Other facts the dossier may use

These are stated in the text and are recorded here only so a later item does not have to read the paper again.

1. Times are UTC. The tornado "begins producing EF0 damage in northern Newcastle at approximately 1956 UTC" (Results, Fig. 1). The KTLX scans the paper discusses are at 1955:27, 1959 and 2016:43 UTC, and the range from KTLX to the tornado fell from 30.6 km at 1955:27 to 11.9 km at 2033:46 (section "Radar data").
2. The merged ground and aerial survey gives a path 23.25 km long with a maximum width of 1.74 km over south Oklahoma City and western Moore (Results and Summary). The Summary adds a damaged area of 19.04 km².
3. Footnote 3 says EF0 damage "consistent with weak tornadic rotation" was seen up to 5.2 km southwest of the start of the path in Fig. 1.
4. The count of structures rated in both surveys differs inside the paper. The abstract says 4,039 and the section "Comparison between aerial and ground damage surveys" says 4,077. The count of EF5 structures is 11 in that section and 10 in the summary. This record keeps both pairs as published.
5. The section "Quantifying damage risk" says catastrophic damage to homes rated EF2 or higher would be expected across "over 25% of the total damage area", and the Summary says "Only 25%" of the damage area is rated EF2 or higher. This record keeps both wordings.
6. The images are credited in the Fig. 3 caption: frames from video by Justin Cox in Fig. 3b and 3d, and a still by Bob Fritchie in Fig. 3f. The acknowledgments also thank Chance Coldiron. The paper states no license for any of them. The publisher's page offers a "Get Permissions" link for the article, which was not followed. This record supplies no rights.
7. The section "Damage survey" gives an address for the aerial survey imagery, `http://meteorology.lyndonstate.edu/vortex2/Moore2013`. It was not opened for this record.

## What this changes

Nothing a visitor sees. The record supports the limit the dossier already states, that a reported damage length or width is not the size of the visible funnel, with a paper that measured both on this tornado. As the tornado approached Briarwood Elementary School at 2016:43 UTC, the funnel was about a fifth of the damage width. It also supports the charter rule that a funnel width must never be taken from a damage width, for this tornado directly and through the earlier studies the paper cites. The paper does not speak to taking a width from a rating.

## Limits

1. The funnel sizes come from one camera at a time, and their ranges assume the funnel sat on the surveyed centerline. The paper says triangulation was not possible.
2. The figures were not read, so this record cannot give a funnel diameter in metres or say which photograph sized which drawn funnel.
3. The text was read from an archived capture, not from the publisher, because the publisher refused the request. The capture is dated 2026-10-05.
4. No image from the paper is cleared for use. The credited photographers and the absence of a stated license are recorded, nothing more.
