# What the Blackwell bulletins establish

The Blackwell chapter now adds two archival document images and reading aids to explain forecast communication before the nighttime tornado. They show regional severe-weather language, not a registered tornado track or evidence that a particular resident received a warning.

Selected source portions and the complete available images were inspected by an agent on October 4, 2026 UTC. No new video, camera registration or original newspaper review is claimed.

## The day's weather and the first bulletin

The [NWS Norman outbreak account](https://www.weather.gov/oun/events-19550525), Summary, describes damaging winds in Kay County during the morning, followed by further storm development that afternoon. Its morning wind estimates are eyewitness estimates, not newly reviewed instrument measurements. The existing chapter retains the disagreements between its Blackwell clock labels and those in the reproduced historical report.

[NWS Wichita's archival collection](https://www.weather.gov/ict/udall) links the [first bulletin](https://www.weather.gov/images/ict/wxstory/udall/warning1.jpg). Its additional severe-thunderstorm area explicitly names Blackwell, Winfield and areas eastward. That text supplies the Blackwell connection. The archive's Udall title alone would not.

The surviving image discusses morning storms, rain amounts and an Arkansas River observation at 11.15 AM. Its opening eastern-Kansas and Missouri warning paragraph ends at noon. That period must not be assigned to the later Blackwell-area addition. No issue date, issuing-office name or time-zone label is established in the crop. The archival collection supplies the May 25, 1955 event context. Morning conditions do not establish a precisely timed morning issuance.

## A later broad forecast and its caption

The [second image](https://www.weather.gov/images/ict/wxstory/udall/warning2.jpg) contains a typewritten severe-weather forecast and a later archive caption. Its body describes possible tornadoes across a large region using Dodge City, Amarillo, Wichita Falls, Ardmore and Wichita. It requests immediate broadcast. The prose is not converted into a warning polygon, and its city list is not presented as an explicit Blackwell warning.

The typewritten heading has May 25 and an overtyped 4.36 PM issue label. Its forecast period is 4.30 PM to 10 PM. The later caption supplies the year 1955, gives 4:36 P.M. and reports that local media broadcast it on the 5:00 news. These are distinct source roles. The broadcast assertion comes from that caption, not an independently obtained station record. Neither layer supplies a time-zone label or establishes reception in Blackwell. No absolute time or tornado-warning lead time is derived.

## Scope and verification

The chapter supplies summaries beside the images because a small teletype scan is not text-accessible by itself. Those summaries identify visible claims and important omissions. They are not a checked verbatim transcript of every character or an account of what Blackwell residents heard.

The reviewed promotion adds two sources, two media items and a section route. It preserves every prior observation, source/media row, event association, clock and immutable dossier snapshot. No historical appearance interval is added. Browser journeys require separate executed evidence. This source record does not establish physical-phone acceptance, reader comprehension or actual warning receipt.
