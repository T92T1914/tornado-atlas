## What the two images contribute

The NOAA image puts the storm in a regional setting. The city point is a supplied annotation. It does not resolve a ground funnel or damage boundary. The caption reports 22:15 UTC on April 27, 2011. The article's displayed date is not treated as established original publication timing because the body is retrospective. [Original NOAA account and PNG](https://www.nesdis.noaa.gov/news/the-tuscaloosa-birmingham-ef-4-tornado).

The NASA article dates the EO-1 ALI acquisition to May 2 and publication to May 5. Its labeled display distinguishes a tan debris track from a bright contrail and identifies nearby landmarks, north and scale. The credited image creators are Jesse Allen and Robert Simmon using NASA EO-1 team data. Michon Scott wrote the caption. The wider download has a different extent and lacks the display's labels. These are separate source variants, not interchangeable resolutions of one framed view. [Original NASA article](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/tornado-track-in-tuscaloosa-alabama-50434/).

The exhibit keeps source colors and annotations. It introduces no guessed point, raster overlay, measurement, force, wind, rating or building-failure time. Neither view is a before-image for the other. The written survey progression remains the route through the damage evidence.
