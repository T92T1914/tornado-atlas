# Reuse terms of the Yearbook of Meteorological Disasters in China

The mainland China database of Zhang and colleagues takes its place names, time notes and damage descriptions from the Yearbook of Meteorological Disasters in China. Its Parameters sheet names the yearbook as the source of the time notes and the Chinese locations and says the English locations are translated from it. The China source browser leaves all of those columns out until the yearbook's own terms are read. This record reads what could be found of them in one session.

No page a session can read publishes reuse terms for the yearbook. The publisher's websites carry only a general notice that reserves all rights and forbids copying without permission, and its knowledge platform says its databases need a separate purchase or an authorization. Nothing grants reuse, and nothing names the yearbook's place names or time notes. So under Decided 52 the place names and time notes stay out of the browser.

This record picks nothing and adds no field. Nothing here is legal advice. Whether a short place name or a time note copied from a yearbook is a work that copyright reaches at all is a legal question this record does not answer.

## The yearbook and its publisher

The yearbook is an annual series in Chinese, 中国气象灾害年鉴, published by 气象出版社, China Meteorological Press. The database README quoted in the [international reuse terms record](international-reuse-terms-2026-11-21.md) names it as the source of its text for 2003 to 2019. No edition of the yearbook was read for this record, and no copyright page of a printed edition was seen.

## What this means for the museum

The CC BY 4.0 license of the database covers the compilation of Zhang and colleagues. It cannot grant what the authors did not hold, and the text they took from the yearbook is the part this question is about. The yearbook's publisher states no terms that would allow the museum to show that text, and its only general notice reserves all rights. Under Decided 52 the place names and time notes stay out of the China browser.

Three routes could change that, and each is a decision for T. A written permission could be asked of China Meteorological Press. A copyright page of a printed edition could be read, if one can be obtained. Or someone qualified could answer whether short place names and time notes are protected at all, which this record does not try to answer.

## Limits

1. Terms can change at the same address. These pages were read at the times given.
2. The record read the publisher's web pages, not a printed edition. A printed edition's copyright page could say more.
3. A web search can miss a page. Five searches in two languages found none, which shows only that none was found, not that none exists.
4. Nothing here judges the database's own license, which the [international reuse terms record](international-reuse-terms-2026-11-21.md) reads.
