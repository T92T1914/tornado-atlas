# Two later views of the damaged landscape

The Joplin chapter now places a satellite aftermath image beside the existing
NWS survey figure. Tuscaloosa adds a separately processed Landsat scar rendering
beside the GOES and EO-1 views. These images help readers connect close-up damage
with a landscape view while keeping each source's interpretation visible.
They are documentary media, not registered footprints or historical appearance.
Source baseline: `4568682e6a7bc7363af5d99e1de36c0f1f6bc587`.

## Joplin: a dated aftermath display

The [NASA Earth Observatory item](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/tornado-in-joplin-missouri-50755/)
identifies a May 30, 2011 Terra/ASTER acquisition. Its article is dated May 31,
with a later page update of June 12, 2026. Those are separate clocks. The download
label includes midnight, but the account establishes only a date. No acquisition
hour or UTC alignment is adopted.

The caption explains the false-color display: red vegetation, gray buildings and
paved surfaces, white clouds and a light-brown track. Complete-image inspection
found cloud obstruction across parts of the city. Atlas does not identify an
individual building, infer a damage rating from a pixel or measure the track.
The older article's provisional casualty figures are not substituted for the
chapter's separate remembrance sources.

Credit: NASA Earth Observatory image created by Jesse Allen and Robert Simmon,
using data provided courtesy of NASA/GSFC/METI/ERSDAC/JAROS, and U.S./Japan ASTER
Science Team. Source caption by Michon Scott. No endorsement is implied.

The source-linked download returned a 1606 by 1606 JPEG rendition, **381,451
bytes**, SHA-256
`67d01b39991050aa409f8e5ff24156873b6ff6342b072be83bd986455c1ec0f0`.
The current link uses NASA's dynamic-image route with clip and size parameters.
The actual received rendition, rather than the page-advertised 2.31 MB object,
is preserved byte for byte. It contains no exposed EXIF or other image metadata.
It is not raw ASTER data or an original instrument product.

The [NASA media-use guidance](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/)
was inspected for educational and informational exhibit/web use, attribution,
non-endorsement and separately identified third-party copyright. The selected
item and full received image identify no separate copyright restriction. The
complete joint data credit remains visible. This decision covers the attributed
educational display; it does not declare every ASTER product public domain or
imply NASA reviewed Atlas's interpretation.

## Tuscaloosa: a scar rendering with an unknown clock

The [USGS media item](https://www.usgs.gov/media/images/tuscaloosa-birmingham-tornado-scar-april-2011)
describes Landsat imagery associated with the Tuscaloosa-Birmingham destruction
trail. Its exact Sources/Usage field says Public Domain. Office of Communications
is the credited publisher; the individual image maker is not named.

Its original link returned a complete **1108 by 577 PNG**, **789,555 bytes**,
SHA-256 `af3e08af2f69d9b6d6f8ea43de1850fe23aac4cc969636831e2fb8a27c8fff6c`.
The retained pixels show a warm diagonal band among colored patches and dark
river shapes. All pixels are opaque. The source's color profile and XMP are
preserved; inspected XMP contains only pixel dimensions. The HTTP modification
date is a server-file date, not an acquisition clock.

There is no printed legend, scale, acquisition date or named landmark. The page's
April 27, 2011 label cannot establish the satellite acquisition date. Atlas does
not give the warm colors a physical meaning, claim a larger extent than EO-1,
locate the tornado, recover a complete 80-mile footprint or register the views
together. Its useful contribution is a distinct source rendering and an
explanation of why association alone cannot supply those measurements.

## Inspection, preservation and visitor acceptance

The two new item identities were checked against current media, earlier research
and rejected-source records before retrieval. Each newly linked still was
requested once, on October 7, 2026 UTC, with a 5 MB and 30-second bound. Both
returned HTTP 200. Their total retained size is 1,171,006 bytes. Author and
independent reviewer inspected both complete images and item-specific reuse
bases. No video interval, audio, map registration or pixel measurement was
added. No previously held source was retried.

Each chapter uses the existing local-image viewer, with its own credit, source,
reuse and clock labels. Original local image links and the complete readable
account remain available without JavaScript. Source cards and immutable dossier
history retain the earlier observations, county records and unregistered
reconstruction contract. A browser test must exercise those actual consumers,
including image failure, return navigation and narrow layouts. A source review
or local browser fixture alone does not establish deployed visitor acceptance.

### Research-to-decision record

- **Question:** Can new satellite evidence explain the existing damage accounts?
- **Current behavior:** Joplin has ground damage and radar; Tuscaloosa has GOES,
  EO-1 and ground aftermath. Neither selected new primary item was present.
- **Earlier work checked:** Current dossiers, media, satellite research and held
  source records at the baseline above. Existing foundations are preserved.
- **Primary portion inspected:** Complete item captions, credits, date labels,
  download links, selected reuse policy and complete received image renditions.
- **Limits:** No raw instrument data, inspected motion, acquisition hour,
  georegistration, calibrated scale or historical wind field.
- **Proposal:** Publish the exact educational displays with separate narrative,
  source identity and accessible image/source routes.
- **Inputs/budget:** Two bounded still retrievals, existing archive projection
  and viewers; no paid service, new engine or native installation.
- **Deciding result:** Reject conflicting rights or unsubstantiated geography;
  require preserved predecessors and functional labels, navigation and failure.
- **Visitor effect:** See the landscape context and understand what the display
  can establish independently of a surveyed route or radar return.
- **Decision:** Implement, with the USGS extent claim narrowed after review.
- **Evidence obtained:** Primary text, exact returned bytes, two complete-image
  inspections and independent rights review. Delivery checks remain separate.
