# Joplin documentary foundation

September 21, 2026

Joplin is the second documentary chapter. I want the warning sequence, the
physical damage and the human account to sit together. This first version has
an original source map, two credited photographs, a seven-entry chronology and
a reading structure. It is not a finished reconstruction.

## Claim and source record

The assessment PDF is preserved in `exhibits/joplin-2011/`. The accompanying
manifest records its hash and those of the displayed assets. PDF pages 7 and 8

extracted text. This does not claim every page was visually inspected.

## Media and provenance

* [Daniel Friskey's storm photograph](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Joplin,_Missouri_tornado_of_2011.jpg)
  has a CC BY-SA 4.0 license on its source page. The accepted Commons permission
  record is visible there; private permission correspondence was not read.
  The photograph is unmodified. Its exact camera location and event time have
  not been registered. A source page date or upload time does not establish a
  synchronized camera clock.
* [The NWS Springfield damage photograph](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:22_May_2011_Joplin_tornado_damage.jpg)
  is preserved through Commons, which identifies it as government work. Its
  original NOAA link is no longer available. The file metadata gives May 23,
  2011 at 13:19, while its description uses the May 22 event date. No local time
  zone, precise location, building identity, damage indicator or death is inferred.
* The map is the original embedded image from Figure 1 of the NWS assessment.
  It is a historical report figure, not a new geographic dataset. It is not
  stretched into a georeferenced layer. The report and underlying map may
  contain third-party components; the software's license does not cover them.

## Remaining work

1. Read the full NIST technical report and record the support for each expanded
   building case study. The overview is not a substitute for that work.
2. Check individual names against the city memorial and independent records.
   Do not infer who died inside an unidentified photographed structure.
3. Obtain original survey geometry with its source and rating definitions.
   Keep county segments separate and retain their original values.
4. Register original footage against visible clocks and landmarks before
   creating a timed reconstruction. Unknown intervals should remain unknown.
5. Add warning polygons and radar only with preserved issue/valid times.

The catalogue now links its reviewed Jasper and Newton County aliases to this
chapter. It does not merge the source records or add their death counts.

## September 23 chronology registration

The original NWS assessment URL was reopened. PDF pages 8 and 9 (printed pages
2 and 3) were rendered from the preserved PDF and visually inspected, including
the full seven-row chronology and the following warning map. The new chronology
package preserves each printed CDT clock label. Its UTC navigation values add
five hours to that explicit offset. The 5:34 p.m. touchdown remains approximate,
without an invented error radius, coordinate or numerical time bound.

The warning map was inspected for context. No warning polygon was traced or
registered, no image was assigned a frame time, and no new third-party asset was
published. Each chronology entry opens PDF page 8 and names the printed-page
locator. The original static account remains available when JavaScript or the
source website fails. The last 5:48 p.m. entry does not imply tornado dissipation.
