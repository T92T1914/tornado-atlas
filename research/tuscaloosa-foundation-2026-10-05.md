# Tuscaloosa and Birmingham: a documentary foundation

The exhibit follows one documented tornado through changing damage and three
retained county records. It explains warning communication and shelter access,
and exposes source differences instead of choosing an unqualified fatality total
or inventing a synchronized visual replay. This is a written exhibit increment.
No photograph, video, radar image or historical appearance has been visually
reviewed or hosted by this contribution.

## Decision and existing material

The selected question was whether the existing Tuscaloosa source queue could
support an actual visitor account through Atlas's current dossier and documentary
page, without a new registry or guessed historical geometry. Source base:
`2d692a7bf183235a4c6aeb034d1ec8962644ddfb`.

The three existing exhibits, archive projection, source-status contract,
documentary styles and current source catalogue were read before writing. Earlier
next-event selection favored Tuscaloosa over Udall for the available survey,
warning and damage accounts. A previously selected NOAA aerial was already
deferred after an HTTP 403 response. It was not retried, replaced through an
alternate host, or represented as inspected pixels. No existing event, immutable
revision or source snapshot was rewritten.

The decision is **implement** a documentary account and one standalone schema 1
dossier input to the existing archive processor. Image hosting and historical
registration remain **deferred**. The work uses the existing source, observation,
clock and place model. It supplies no duplicate media registry or simulator.

## Primary sources and exact inspection

### Birmingham survey

[NWS Birmingham's Tuscaloosa and Birmingham EF4 survey](https://www.weather.gov/bmx/event_04272011tuscbirm)
was inspected as original official text: summary, complete written progression,
approximate start and end coordinates, parent-storm account, warning paragraph,
radar descriptions and damage-image captions. Linked image pixels were not
inspected. The page is undated, so its present contents are not labeled a recovered
2011 revision.

It supports the whole-track EF4 rating, reported 80.68-mile length, maximum
2,600-yard width near Interstate 65, estimated 190 mph wind, approximate endpoints
and named damage progression. These are source-reported survey interpretations,
not new measurements of wind, funnel width, building coordinates or arrival times.
The parent supercell's much longer journey included other tornadoes, including
the later separate Ohatchee account. It is not one continuous Tuscaloosa track.

One bounded attempt to retain the original PDF returned HTTP 403 before a body
was saved. It was not retried or routed through another downloader. No report
pixels, figure rendering, image derivative or full-file hash was obtained.
Accessible original text supports the attributed narrative, while visual
acceptance remains absent.

The survivor interviews were conducted 10 to 13 days after the outbreak across
affected states. They were not random or representative, did not use a structured
questionnaire, and did not directly interview deceased victims' families or
hospitalized people with severe injuries. The chapter does not turn their themes
into population frequencies. Outbreak-wide warning averages are not a measured
Tuscaloosa lead time.

## Source differences preserved

The present Birmingham survey lists 65 fatalities. The December 2011 assessment
summary lists 66 for its Tuscaloosa/Jefferson row. The retained 2026 catalogue
revision reports 44 direct and 8 indirect deaths in the Tuscaloosa segment, 20
direct deaths in Jefferson, and none in Greene. The Tuscaloosa narrative discusses
deaths reported in the months afterward. Source date, county scope and
direct/indirect classifications remain attached to those values. No canonical
summed total, named victim register or explanation for every difference has been
established here.

The survey's approximate whole-track interval is 4:43 to 6:14 p.m. CDT. NCEI
retains `CST-6`: 15:43 to 15:48, 15:48 to 16:35, and 16:35 to 17:14. The current
importer converts the combined standard-time interval to 21:43 through 23:14 UTC,
matching the survey's approximate daylight-time endpoints. The assessment's
Table 1 labels its clock CST but gives the shorter 3:50 to 4:45 p.m. interval.
That scope difference remains unresolved. Neither agreement at endpoints nor
an unverified summary interval registers a camera or a neighborhood arrival time.

The survey and NCEI narrative also use different approximate distances from
Tarrant when describing the ending. The reported coordinates remain unchanged.
No newly measured position is supplied to reconcile the descriptions.

## Attribution, reuse and visual limits

[NWS government and third-party content guidance](https://www.weather.gov/disclaimer)
was read at its government-data and third-party-data sections. The attributed
written account and source links preserve agency identity and do not imply
endorsement. This museum interpretation is not an official NWS product. A
government webpage can carry material with separate rights, so no blanket image
hosting permission is inferred.

The survey's selected aerial remains an unavailable, uninspected lead. The
assessment's Figure 16 caption describes a destroyed wood-frame home in Concord
and credits Gary Woodall, NWS Phoenix. The caption was read, but the image pixels
and any item-specific exceptions were not. No image is added to the public media
array, and no derivative or embed is produced. No footage is described as watched.
Camera clock, position, bearing and scale remain unregistered.

An eventual contextual photograph could explain the difference between city-scale
damage and individual construction outcomes without qualifying as reconstruction
evidence. It still needs actual visual inspection and source-specific attribution
and rights. This is the precise missing visual prerequisite, not an assertion
that relevant images do not exist.

## Visitor and integration acceptance

The written account uses the existing documentary styles and appearance control.
Its six survey stops are understandable without a map. Source and observation
routes resolve through the existing dossier interface. Approximate tornado
endpoints are the only coordinate-bearing observations. Named damage places
have no new point, arrival time or optical registration.

The selected lightweight tests check the real schema 1 validator, current county
continuation and endpoint association, retained clock/impact classifications,
chapter-to-dossier routes, duplicate anchors, and the absence of uninspected media.
A negative case rejects a point added to an unregistered damage observation.
Root integration owns event-index composition, existing archive publication,
final independent review and desktop/mobile browser journeys. Those checks are
not claimed by this source contribution before execution.

A useful next path/damage increment would let a visitor select an attributed
survey stop and read its damage contrast alongside the exact source passage.
Named places, approximate source endpoints and a digitized survey footprint must
remain distinct. This contribution does not geocode those stops, join endpoints
into a survey path, or make a historical wind/structural simulation claim.

## Independent source corrections before integration

The final source review corrected the initial warning interpretation. Printed page 17 uses Talking Rock and Cullman as examples of omitted city names. Printed page 18 presents added Tuscaloosa landmarks as a best practice. The 4:15 p.m. CDT time belongs to issuance for the original supercell. The subsequent second supercell has no exact development time in that passage. The account and dossier now retain that sequence without assigning different tracks. Figure 16 is on printed page 37, PDF page 45. Caption text has been inspected, but its pixels remain uninspected. Earlier draft wording is retained in Git history rather than treated as an accepted source conclusion.
