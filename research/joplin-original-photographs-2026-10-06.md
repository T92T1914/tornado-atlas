# Joplin: two photographs, different evidence

The chapter already hosted these two original photographs. This increment adds their source and role records to the existing dossier and makes both available through the shared image viewer. It does not add a new image, a camera position, a synchronized video or a historical appearance interval.

## The storm photograph

The complete retained 1557 by 932 pixel image was visually inspected on October 6, 2026. Satellite dishes and vehicles fill the foreground. Behind them is a dark storm, with a bright flash at the left. The [Commons file page](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Joplin,_Missouri_tornado_of_2011.jpg) attributes it to Daniel Friskey, associates it with the May 22, 2011 Joplin tornado and describes a power flash. Its file-history row names uploader WikiSal and a March 21, 2026 upload. These are separate roles and dates.

The page lists CC BY-SA 4.0 and a public VRT notice recording permission review. The private correspondence was not accessed. [The license terms](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/) require attribution, a license link and indication of changes, with applicable share-alike terms for adaptations. Atlas preserves the existing image unchanged and retains its source and license links. This record does not qualify the Facebook video linked by Commons, which was not inspected.

The description's event date conflicts with a structured-data inception value of March 21, 2025. Neither that field nor the later upload is a calibrated capture time. The exact camera position is unregistered. One flash and one silhouette cannot establish a continuous outline, funnel width, wind speed or an impact time.

## The aftermath photograph

The complete retained 1024 by 768 pixel image was visually inspected on October 6, 2026. Broken timber, insulation and bent metal occupy the foreground beside a road and a damaged sign frame. The [Commons file page](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:22_May_2011_Joplin_tornado_damage.jpg) attributes the image to NWS Springfield, Missouri, names uploader Wxtrackercody and marks the item PD-US-NOAA-NWS. That is the existing item-specific attributed reuse basis, rather than a blanket claim that all government-hosted imagery is public domain. The original legacy NOAA page was not newly inspected, and no individual photographer or separate rights holder is invented.

The description uses the May 22 event date. Camera metadata instead says May 23 at 13:19, with its clock and time zone unverified. The December 8, 2013 upload is a third date. The scene has not been located. Debris does not establish its original structure, anchorage, failure order, individual EF rating or a person's death site. These photographs are separate views, not a registered before-and-after pair.

## Visibility and warnings

The selected warning passages in [NIST's retrospective](https://www.nist.gov/feature-stories/joplin-tornado-calamity-and-boon-resilience-10-years), under Emergency Communication Is Key, were read on October 6, 2026. NIST describes visual confirmation obstructed by rain and coverage focused on a northern storm, within its account of 140 survivor interviews. This is an attributed historical synthesis. The smaller NWS case-study sample already in the chapter keeps its own methods and limitations. The accounts do not establish every resident's view or explain an individual death.

The photograph and the retrospective answer related questions without registering one another. The image supplies a visible scene. The interviews explain reported information-seeking and visibility barriers. The existing chronology retains source-reported alert times. None fills a missing optical clock or camera calibration.
