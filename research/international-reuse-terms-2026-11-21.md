# Reuse terms of three international tornado datasets

The roadmap asks for the reuse terms of international research datasets before any further adapter. This record reads the terms of the three datasets that the next regions would most likely draw on: Europe, northern Eurasia including the former Soviet Union, and mainland China. Japan already has its own adapter and is not repeated here.

Only one of the three is under an open license. The mainland China database is published under CC BY 4.0, though part of its text is copied from a yearbook whose own terms are not read here. The agreement for the European Severe Weather Database forbids publishing its raw data without a written exception. The northern Eurasia database is a supplement to a journal article whose publisher holds its copyright and says that posting it in a searchable database needs written permission.

This record picks no source and adds no adapter or import. Which dataset joins the collection stays with the research desk. Nothing here is legal advice. Where a term leaves a question open, the record says so and does not answer it.

Point viii allows derived works "in a scientific context (like a formal scientific publication or a contribution to a scientific conference)" with the source line `Source: European Severe Weather Database, ESSL (eswd.eu).` Point ix requires that no third party can recreate the original data from a derived product "with reasonable accuracy". Every other point quoted above reads the same in both versions.

What this means for the museum. A catalogue browser of ESWD records like the NOAA and JMA browsers would publish raw records, which point iv forbids. A public museum website is not clearly the scientific context that point viii describes by its examples of a formal publication or a conference contribution. Such a browser would need a written exception from ESSL given in advance. Signing the agreement, asking for an exception or paying a fee would each be a decision for T, and none of them is taken here. The conditions page of the observer site, at `https://www.eswd.eu/ewob/tac.php`, answered 403 at 2026-11-22 00:59:53 UTC and again at 06:18:01 UTC with an empty body, so it is named here and not linked, and it was not read.

The [repository's record](https://data.4tu.nl/v2/articles/19801633) was retrieved at 2026-11-22 00:59:49 UTC, 6,259 bytes, SHA-256 `633c7a77bc334ac4c28591d14d23be4710584e8fd5cfe6ffe7e0c07d79034864`. It gives the license as `CC BY 4.0` with the URL of the [license deed](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/), first online on 2022-05-23 and version 2 posted on 2023-04-05. It lists two files, `v.2 Tornado database in China mainland from 2003 to 2019.xls` of 384,000 bytes and `README for version 2.0.pdf` of 68,090 bytes.

The README was retrieved at 2026-11-22 01:00:57 UTC. Its MD5 `7db00da323ea78b4eee2ecc17f1d13dd` matches the value the repository computed, and its SHA-256 is `fd1beb11eacaf33624318102f05c9f2d1930880f372f5c0447c95afada1268b2`. It describes the data this way:

> According to the textual information provided by the Yearbook of Meteorological Disasters in China from 2003 to 2019 and other public media report data, tornadoes occurring in China mainland from 2003 to 2019 were collected in this dataset

What this means for the museum. CC BY 4.0 allows copying, redistribution and adaptation, including in a public browser, as long as the record credits the creators, links the license and says what was changed. The README also sets limits an adapter would have to keep:

1. Longitude and latitude are "derived by geocoding method". They are the authors' placement of a place name, not a surveyed point, and a browser would have to say so.
2. A recorded date such as `2018-03-04/2018-03-05` "indicates the approximate date range when the tornado occurred, not the duration of the tornado".
3. Start and end times are text in UTC+8, including words such as "afternoon" and "night", and must stay as recorded.
4. The README does not say who assigned the EF rating of each case or on what evidence.
5. The yearbook is published by others. The license covers the authors' compilation. Whether the Chinese damage descriptions copied from the yearbook carry other terms is not settled here. The [yearbook reuse terms record](yearbook-reuse-terms-2026-11-22.md) of 2026-11-22 reads what the yearbook's publisher states.

The data file itself was not downloaded, so its row count and columns are not checked in this record.

## Limits

1. Each term is quoted from the page or document retrieved at the time given. Terms can change at the same address, and the ESWD agreement has already moved to a new one, so an adapter must read them again from the publisher's current page first.
2. The AMS pages came back with a different SHA-256 on a second request two minutes later at the same or nearly the same size, which suggests content that changes with each request. Their hashes identify the copy read for this record and not a stable revision. The ESSL agreement and the 4TU record gave the same hash both times.
3. The ESWD observer conditions page refused the request and was not read.
4. Neither the northern Eurasia supplement nor the China data file was downloaded, so no count in this record comes from the data itself.
5. This record reads three datasets. Other regional catalogues, national weather service archives and the Canadian and Australian records were not searched.
