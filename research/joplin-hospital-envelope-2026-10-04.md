# Joplin: the frame, the windows and a working hospital

The new chapter increment answers a specific question: how could the West Tower at St. John's Regional Medical Center remain standing and still lose its function as a hospital? The standing structure, damaged enclosure and working interior need separate evidence. The photographs make that distinction visible beside the investigation's findings.

## What was inspected

The source is the [March 2014 NIST NCSTAR 3 report](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-C13-a0ac8adb5269166f1b1e230423cf79ec/pdf/GOVPUB-C13-a0ac8adb5269166f1b1e230423cf79ec.pdf#page=160). Its retained Government Publishing Office copy is 39,727,397 bytes, with SHA-256 `0b1c41b9134f74f2e4e600e68b27d3197598f5c42e88ea7c2ad6cc8e715cca78`. That identity matches the earlier acquisition receipt. The complete report was not acquired again or added to the public tree.

On October 4, 2026 UTC, an agent visually inspected PDF page 4 and pages 160 to 163, corresponding to the copyright statement and printed pages 96 to 99. These pages were rendered at 150 dpi. The complete embedded photograph streams from Figures 3-9 and 3-10 were also inspected at their available size. Extracted text from the hospital overview on PDF pages 156 and 158 and the continuation on page 164 was read. Text extraction is separate from visual page inspection. This is not a full report or complete hospital-case review.

NIST reports no structural damage to the West Tower's concrete frame or floor slabs. It reports significant envelope damage and identifies wind pressure and debris impact as the likely combined causes of the window damage. The openings allowed wind, water and debris into the interior. On printed page 99, NIST explains why the resulting loss of environmental control, lighting and elevator movement would have prevented function even without the power outage. Those are the investigation's findings, not measurements inferred from the photographs.

The fifth-floor window comparison retains the report's limitations. NIST identifies the Behavioral Health Unit windows as breakage resistant but lacked their renovation drawings and specifications. The two views do not establish a controlled material comparison, a time sequence, a wind speed or current construction advice. Capture times and camera positions remain unregistered. No fatality location or new fatality accounting is added.
