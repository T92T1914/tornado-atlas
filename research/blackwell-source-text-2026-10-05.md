## What the marked readings preserve

The chapter uses native details blocks with a separate reading key for each document. Uppercase source wording, readable typing errors and useful line grouping remain. Spacing is normalized. The text uses the surrounding reading font and wraps to the available width rather than shrinking to imitate the teletype image. This is a presentation choice, not a reconstruction of the original page's typography.

Square brackets are editorial markers, not source words. `[unclear word]`, `[unclear text]` and `[unclear character]` identify unresolved portions of the first image. `[overtyped text]` identifies overlapping characters in the second. `[dots]` marks repeated punctuation whose precise count is not reproduced. No missing text is completed from expected grammar or historical context.

The first reading retains `DEN N` without expanding it into an issuing-office identity. Its word following `SEVERE`, the direction word after `TOPEKA`, a run after `AND`, and a character after `12` remain unresolved. The legible `BEE`, `FRO` and `THAT ARE` wording is retained rather than corrected to expected English. Rain amounts and the river observation are document statements, without added units or clock conversions.

The first document's noon ending belongs to its earlier Kansas and Missouri paragraph. The later area addition explicitly names Blackwell to Winfield and eastward. The source reading does not assign the noon ending to that addition, derive an issue date from collection context or establish who received it.

The second reading preserves the legible `POSSIBILITE` wording. Overtyped runs in the heading after `4.36` and in the city-list paragraph remain marked. The typewritten forecast, including its immediate-broadcast request, is separate from the later archive caption. The caption's year, 4:36 P.M. label and 5:00 News broadcast assertion retain that later source role. They are not an independently checked station log or proof of Blackwell receipt.
