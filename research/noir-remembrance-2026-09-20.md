# Noir reading and documented places

I want the history to read as a complete account, with enough evidence for a
reader to follow a question further. This update adds six longer historical
sections and expands the three existing community discussions. The writing
keeps a distinction between the official record, a research interpretation and
a lead that still needs checking.

The interface now uses a black, white and gray Noir appearance across the
exhibit, catalogue, form study and wind lab. Light and System are available.
Source photographs, radar palettes and scientific wind keys retain their colors.
A saved appearance remains in effect across the four pages.

## What the location means

The first optional marker identifies the approximate TWISTEX vehicle recovery
location. It does not identify an exact place or time of death. Recovery,
initial impact and a memorial are different types of location. The card makes
that distinction before offering a link to the paper.

The coordinate is taken from the later journal account by Wurman and colleagues,
[The Role of Multiple-Vortex Tornado Structure in Causing Storm Researcher
Fatalities](https://doi.org/10.1175/BAMS-D-13-00221.1), printed pages 38 to 40,
the storm-chasing teams account and Figures 6 and 9. The indexed primary-source
location paragraph was inspected. Direct publisher access failed and the full
figure was not visually reviewed. The exhibit states this access limit.

The [preliminary conference paper](https://ams.confex.com/ams/36Radar/webprogram/Manuscript/Paper229323/AMS-Radar-2013-ROTATE13r.pdf)
reports a different latitude. The exhibit follows the later journal account
and discloses the disagreement. Neither the marker nor its screen size supplies
an error radius. No recovery or death locations have been inferred for the
other five people named in remembrance.

## Historical and research sources

The [NWS service assessment](https://www.weather.gov/media/publications/assessments/13oklahoma_tornadoes.pdf)
was read for the environment, tornado and flooding context, and public response.
The environment appears on printed page 5; the May 31 impacts and response are
discussed on printed pages 27 to 36. Traffic problems in the wider region should
not be assigned as the cause of the TWISTEX fatalities.

[Crowdsourcing the El Reno 2013 Tornado](https://doi.org/10.1175/BAMS-D-15-00174.1)
provides a method lead for synchronizing and locating footage. Indexed methods
passages were inspected, not the complete article. This update adds no derived
camera solution, reconstructed shape, or fitted damage coefficient from it.

The community essays explain why a radar velocity measurement, surveyed damage,
and an official rating answer different questions. A forum interpretation is
kept as a question until its underlying evidence can support a specific claim.

Other casualty locations require their own public source and location type.
The longer history is still an expanding exhibit, not a completed documentary
for every storm in the catalogue. Historical 3D reconstruction, validated damage
