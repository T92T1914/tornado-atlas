# Three ways a tornado enters the historical record

These additions connect an interpreted damage map, a later memorial and an investigation photograph to the existing exhibits. They do not add a registered appearance interval, camera position or fatality location.

Selected original images and their source captions were inspected by an agent on October 3, 2026 UTC. The full pages and technical reports were not newly reviewed. A separate request to retain the raw NWS Blackwell HTML returned HTTP 403. It was not retried. The selected historical passages remained readable through the supported web interface. Original image files were acquired separately.

## Blackwell: a survey, a map and a later memorial

[NWS Norman's outbreak account](https://www.weather.gov/oun/events-19550525) describes a building survey published by the Blackwell Journal-Tribune. It reports approximately 85 damaged but occupied buildings, 70 with extensive but repairable damage and 190 completely destroyed. The original newspaper editions have not been inspected here.

The [complete damage map](https://www.weather.gov/images/oun/wxevents/19550525/blackwelldamage.gif) credits its smoothed contours to Doug Speheger, NWS Norman. Its legend identifies newspaper editions from May 29 and May 30, 1955. The red, blue and green regions describe damage classes. They are not wind measurements or precise entries for each building. No coordinates are recovered from the image. Atlas preserves the complete original image and its source legend.

The same NWS page dates the memorial dedication to May 25, 2005 and credits its [memorial close-up](https://www.weather.gov/images/oun/wxevents/19550525/memorial.jpg), Photo 7, to NWS Norman. The printed date remains visible. This is a photograph of remembrance fifty years later. It is not a storm-time observation. The photograph's embedded modification date is not treated as a capture-time calibration. Atlas does not transcribe the inscription into verified individual victim records or assign a death location from the memorial.

## Joplin: an investigation included survivors

The [NIST investigation overview](https://www.nist.gov/disaster-failure-studies/joplin-tornado) credits its opening photograph to NIST and identifies the scene as a National Construction Safety Team member interviewing a survivor. The overview describes a preliminary four-engineer reconnaissance from May 25 to 28, 2011. That paragraph does not establish the photograph's exact date.

The [original available still](https://www.nist.gov/sites/default/files/images/2018/10/12/joplin.jpg) is 288 by 216 pixels. It remains complete, in its original color and displayed no larger than that size. Narrow screens can reduce it to fit. No enhancement or recovered detail is claimed. The caption does not identify either person or a precise location. No inference about their warning or shelter experience is drawn from the image.

This addition does not broaden the earlier selected-page review of school refuge areas, siren response or warning-partner methods. It does not turn those findings into individual interview histories.

## El Reno: distinguish the damage examples

The [NWS event page](https://www.weather.gov/oun/events-20130531) explicitly identifies its damage photographs as work by NWS/NOAA survey personnel. Photo 3 describes all walls collapsed at a house near Rother Road and SW 15th Street and assigns EF3. [Photo 4](https://www.weather.gov/images/oun/wxevents/20130531/damage/damage04.jpg) describes large roof sections removed while most walls remained standing and assigns EF2.

Photo 4 was already hosted in the exhibit. This addition joins that exact file to the archive media registry and adds the caption-level comparison to the historical account. They are different houses after the event. Their proximity does not establish equal construction or wind exposure. Neither is a before and after image of the other. No camera position, capture time, failure order or wind speed is inferred.

Two additional original NWS operations photographs were inspected privately. They show third-party interfaces or television imagery within the photographed screens. They are not hosted in this addition under a blanket government-material claim. That decision does not establish that they can never be reused. Their incidental content needs a separate rights assessment.
