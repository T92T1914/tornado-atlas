# The first siren and a different warned storm

The existing chronology records the first warning and first siren two minutes apart. That proximity does not establish that both concerned the same storm. The [July 2011 NWS assessment, Section 3, printed page 13](https://www.weather.gov/media/publications/assessments/Joplin_tornado.pdf#page=19) attributes the 5:11 p.m. CDT siren activation primarily to funnel-cloud reports west of Joplin. It distinguishes those reports from warning 30, issued two minutes earlier for a different storm affecting western Jasper County and northeastern Joplin. Some interviewees combined the siren with the northern warning details and thought the threat was to the north.

This is the assessment's attributed explanation of the Emergency Manager's decision and selected interviews. [The section's methods and dissemination context, printed pages 11 to 12](https://www.weather.gov/media/publications/assessments/Joplin_tornado.pdf#page=17), describe interviews with warning partners and a local media round-table. They do not establish a representative survey. Section 2's 54-resident sample is not a denominator for all Section 3 interviews.
