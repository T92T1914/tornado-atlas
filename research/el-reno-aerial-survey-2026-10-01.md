# Reading the aerial damage envelope

[Wakimoto and colleagues' 2016 paper](https://doi.org/10.1175/MWR-D-15-0367.1) is a separate source from the 2015 photogrammetric paper already in the dossier. Section 2 on [printed page 1751, PDF page 3](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/32144/noaa_32144_DS1.pdf#page=3) reports ground surveys on June 1 and 3, 2013 and an aerial survey on June 4. The Cessna 172 flew at 300 and 1200 m above ground, and the survey produced 1249 aerial photographs. Ground estimates determined the assigned EF value where the surveys disagreed.

[Printed page 1752, PDF page 4](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/32144/noaa_32144_DS1.pdf#page=4) reports an EF0 damage envelope roughly 7 km wide just east of Highway 81. The authors explicitly say this is not entirely attributable to the El Reno tornado. A separate anticyclonic tornado contributed to the southeast extension, and the southern boundary suggests rear-flank downdraft damage. This envelope does not replace the [NWS maximum tornado width of 2.6 miles](https://www.weather.gov/oun/events-20130531) or final EF3 damage rating.
